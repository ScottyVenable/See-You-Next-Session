Overview & Breakdown
A game where you play as a psychologist or psychiatrist who sees clients in a sit down setting. You have to diagnose and treat clients accurately, and clients present symptoms ranging from easy to very difficult.


The Goal: To diagnose the patient and provide/refer to them the appropriate care.
Mechanical Ideas
1 hour sessions are split into 6 “turns”, 10 minutes each to simulate the intel gathering and clinical support that is given by the professional. Perhaps there is an “action points” system to do certain actions.[a] Lots of unique dialogue per client. The player must, at the end of a session, write “session notes” for the client by filling out a sheet. This sheet may be just dropdowns and short text boxes for them- or if we wanted, could be freely up to the player. We need to be able to extract the data from the session notes to be able to give the player a score and points after each session… (Not designed like a strict puzzle level though).


- Use points to “Gather Patient Presentation” which allows you to use a magnifying glass-like tool that follows the mouse to inspect the patient to look for things like bitten nails, bruises, eye contact, etc.
- Writing Notes: <see audio recording (16:30)>
- When certain things are identified, the game automatically creates an entry in a “Journal”. For example, if the client is fidgeting and the player chooses to select Observe Mannerisms then a scribble sound effect is made and the Journal tab is highlighted (showing a new entry has been discovered and added). When checked it says “I noticed that the client was fidgeting” certain words will be bolded and color coated based on the type of discovery that is (mannerism, speech, etc.) when hovering over it, it might give a quick look at some data for that behavior. When clicked it goes to the symptom in the Handbook showing possible diagnosis’, causes, things to look for, etc. (same with diagnosis’)
- Players will complete the session notes by cross referencing with the Patient Journal


Rewards for completing a session/maybe accurately writing documentation, could be knowledge points that can be used to “upgrade” the player[b] such as new features, abilities.

MVP Document
MVP Design Document
1. High-Level Concept
* Genre: Narrative Simulation / Puzzle
* Elevator Pitch: A game where you play as a mental health professional. You must manage your mental energy (Focus) to spot contradictions between what a patient says and how they present, diagnosing them accurately before the session ends.
* Inspirations: Papers, Please (Document handling/Vibe), Ace Attorney (Finding contradictions).
________________


2. The Core Loop
The gameplay consists of 1-Hour Sessions split into 4 Turns (15 minutes each).
A. The Mechanics
1. Dialogue (Listening):
* The patient speaks via text box.
* Drag-and-Drop System: Players click important keywords in the dialogue (e.g., "I haven't slept") and drag them to their Clipboard. These become Text Tokens.
2. Observation (Looking) - Uses "Focus":
* Focus Meter: A limited resource bar (replacing Action Points).
* The Mechanic: Player activates "Focus Mode" (screen vignettes/dims).
* The Action: Hovering over the patient reveals physical cues (e.g., bitten nails, dilated pupils).
* The Cost: Finding a clue costs Focus.
* The Reward: Dragging a visual clue to the Clipboard creates a Visual Token.
3. Synthesis (The Puzzle):
* The Handbook: A reference book on the desk listing disorders and criteria.
* Matching: Drag a Token onto a Handbook entry to test a theory.
* Contradictions: Dragging a Visual Token (e.g., "Normal Reflexes") on top of a conflicting Text Token (e.g., "My leg is paralyzed") creates a Breakthrough (and restores Focus).
4. Documentation (End of Session):
* The player does not type notes. Instead, the "Session Note" is a form where you drag your collected Tokens into slots (e.g., "Observed Symptoms," "Patient Stated," "Final Diagnosis").

4. UI Layout (The "Desk")
* Left Side: The Patient (2D Character Art).
* Center Bottom: Dialogue Box (Draggable Text).
* Right Side (The Workstation):
   * Clipboard: Where you store/combine Tokens.
   * The Handbook: Reference for diagnosis.
   * Focus Meter: Visual bar indicating mental energy.
   * Clock: Shows which "Turn" (15-min block) you are on.
________________


5. Progression & Economy
* Scoring: Based on accurate diagnosis + correct identification of symptoms in notes.
* Rewards: "Knowledge Points" (KP).
* Upgrades: Spend KP to decorate the office.
   * Functional Decor Idea: Buying a comfortable chair increases Max Focus
   * Buying a fish tank slows down the patient's anxiety meter.
